\begin{abstract}
  Synthetic algebraic geometry is a new approach to algebraic geometry
  that studies the Zariski topos through an internal language.
  This language is based on homotopy type theory augmented with a
  postulated commutative ring $R$ satisfying three axioms.
  Work in progress towards a synthetic version of Serre Duality
  lead us to study a class of modules that we call the \emph{finitely adequate $R$-modules}.

  In this article, we show that these finitely adequate $R$-modules have many rich properties.
  They form an abelian subcategory of the category of all $R$-modules
  that is closed under extensions and carries a closed symmetric monoidal structure,
  with the internal hom being the usual $\Hom_R$ of $R$-modules.
  We also prove that dualization is an anti-equivalence on this subcategory,
  and characterize its injective and projective modules.

  We also study the geometry of $R$-modules via their tangent spaces, and consider
  a variety of properties that $R$-modules may have, establishing many implications between them.
  We end by giving a large number of examples of $R$-modules that illustrate the diversity
  that can arise in this constructive setting,
  and use these examples to show that many implications between these properties do not hold.
\end{abstract}
