The first motivating question behind this article is whether synthetic algebraic geometry admits a notion similar to the coherent sheaves in classical algebraic geometry. Coherent sheaves share properties with finite dimensional vector spaces and appear for example in Serre duality, an important tool of classical algebraic geometry.

In synthetic algebraic geometry, we work with a generalization of $\mathcal O_X$ sheaves called $R$-module \emph{bundles}, where $R$ is the base ring of synthetic algebraic geometry. This ring $R$ has many surprising properties which come from working synthetically. Module bundles are just maps $X\to \Mod{R}$, assigning to each point of a type $X$ an $R$-module. In \cite{draft}, the abelian category of \emph{weakly quasi coherent} $R$-modules was defined and bundles of such modules on schemes were used for cohomological calculations. While these bundles are more general than the usual quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$-modules, it was still possible to adapt many proofs from the literature. Similarly, a synthetic replacement for coherent sheaves should admit the same arguments used in known proofs. Any direct adaption of the definition of coherent sheaves is bound to fail: Classically, it only works well if finitely presented modules over the base ring form an abelian category, which is known to be false for the base ring in synthetic algebraic geometry.

Let $X$ be a smooth projective scheme. To prove a basic version of the Serre duality theorem (following, e.g., \cite{Hartshorne}), which covers useful examples, we would ideally need one subcategory $\mathcal A$ of the $R$-module bundles on $X$ and one subcategory $\mathcal A^\prime\subseteq \Mod{R}$ with the following properties:

\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Locally free sheaves are contained in $\mathcal A$.
\item For any $\mathcal F$ in $\mathcal A$ all cohomology modules $H^i(X,\mathcal F)$ are in $\mathcal A^\prime$.
\item The dualization functor given by $\Hom_R(\argmhole,R)$ is exact on $\mathcal{A}^\prime$.
\end{enumerate}

Our initial hope was that $\mathcal A^\prime$ can be the category of \emph{finitely adequate $R$-modules}, which we describe below and $\mathcal A$ can be chosen as bundles on $X$ with values in $\mathcal A^\prime$. In work in progress based on this article it turned out, that $\mathcal A$ has to be chosen as the strictly smaller category of vector bundles on $X$ in order for the cohomology to take values in finitely adequate modules.

Since the base ring in synthetic algebraic geometry is not coherent, we cannot mimic the classical definition of coherent sheaves.
It was already seen in~\cite{diffgeo-article} that there is a duality between finitely presented $R$-modules and finitely \emph{co}presented $R$-modules, where a module is finitely copresented if it merely is the kernel of a map between finite free modules.
In this article we extend this duality to \emph{finitely adequate} $R$-modules,
which we define as $R$-modules that are merely the kernel of a linear map between finitely presented $R$-modules.
These finitely adequate $R$-modules turn out to form an abelian category $\finadeq$ with many good and surprising properties:

\begin{itemize}
\item The module $\Hom_R(M, N)$ is finitely adequate when $M$ and $N$ are.
\item Dualization is an equivalence $\Hom_R(\argmhole, R) : (\finadeq)^{\op} \to \finadeq$.
\item The injective modules are exactly the finitely presented modules and the projective modules are exactly the finitely copresented modules.
\item Finitely adequate $R$-modules are closed under extensions (inside $\Mod{R}$).
\item The category $\finadeq$ of finitely adequate modules is closed symmetric monoidal, with tensor product the double dual of the usual tensor product.
\end{itemize}

In \cite{adelman-construction} Adelman constructs the free abelian category on a given additive category. Our definition of finitely adequate $R$-modules is quite similar to this construction and satisfies a characterization of free abelian categories related to property (ii). For this reason, we considered calling finitely adequate $R$-modules ``Adelman modules'', but decided to name them after the existing notion of adequate modules \cite[Chapter 46]{stacks-project}, once we were aware of it.

The original motivation mentioned in the beginning was to show that Serre duality holds for $R$-module bundles on $\bP^n$ that are finitely adequate at all points. From ongoing work building on this article we know that this is not possible. But it is true that the cohomology of vector bundles on $\bP^n$ takes values in finitely adequate $R$-modules and it is possible to adapt Hartshorne's proof of Serre duality to this situation. There is work in progress on extending this to Serre duality for vector bundles on general smooth projective schemes. We do not know of an existing constructive proof or any proof over a general ring.

In \cite{diffgeo-article} a definition of tangent spaces at any point of a general type was defined. For property (iii) above, a surprising property of finitely adequate $R$-modules was used: They are always isomorphic to their own tangent space equipped with a canonical $R$-module structure. We call $R$-modules with this property \emph{tangential} and study this and the related property of being \emph{infinitesimally linear} in \Cref{infinitesimally-linear-section}.

\Cref{adequate-section} defines finitely adequate $R$-modules and shows that they have the properties mentioned above. We use that finitely adequate $R$-modules are tangential to show that they are closed under extensions.

In \Cref{examples-section}, we show by various examples and counterexamples the properties of $R$-modules mentioned in this article relate to each other. We also show that the tensor product of finitely adequate $R$-modules is not always finitely adequate, explaining the need for the use of the double dualization in the symmetric monoidal structure mentioned above.
