\title{Cohomology in Synthetic Stone Duality}

\begin{document}

\author{Felix Cherubini, Thierry Coquand, Freek Geerligs and Hugo Moeneclaey}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Clausen and Scholze \cite{Scholze} introduced light condensed sets, the sheaves on the site of light profinite sets, as an alternative to topological spaces.
Synthetic Stone duality, introduced in \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}, extends homotopy type theory by four axioms that hold in the higher topos of light condensed anima, and so gives a language for reasoning internally about it. In that work it was shown that $H^1(S,\Z) = 0$ for $S$ a Stone space, that $H^1(X,\Z)$ for $X$ compact Hausdorff can be computed by \v{C}ech cohomology, and that $H^1(\mathbb{I},\Z) = 0$ for the unit interval $\mathbb{I}$.
We extend these results to cohomology in all degrees, with coefficients in families of countably presented abelian groups that may vary over the space; in degree $1$ the coefficient groups need not be abelian. These are synthetic analogues of results of Dyckhoff \cite{dyckhoff76,dyckhoff76-1}, which Scholze uses to compare condensed and sheaf cohomology of compact Hausdorff spaces, and here they are obtained by elementary internal reasoning. As a consequence, deloopings of countably presented groups, and more generally Postnikov complete types with countably presented homotopy groups, are local with respect to the unit interval.
The main tool is a form of Scott continuity, which also allows us to show that all the axioms of Barton and Commelin's condensed type theory \cite{bc24} hold in synthetic Stone duality.
\end{abstract}
