The goal of this section is to prove that the axioms of Barton-Commelin's condensed type theory hold in synthetic Stone duality. Sections \ref{stone-spaces} and \ref{overtly-discrete-types} contain generalities on Stone spaces and overtly discrete types. \Cref{scott-continuity-for-cohomology} contains the proof of Barton-Commelin's axioms restricted to Stone spaces, which is the only case required for our cohomoloogy computations. \Cref{barton-commelin} contains the proof of the axioms proper, meaning it extends results from \Cref{scott-continuity-for-cohomology} to compact Hausdorff spaces. It can be skipped by readers only interested in the cohomology results.



\subsection{Stone spaces}
\label{stone-spaces}

We recall generalities about Stone spaces. Most results come from \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}. 

\begin{definition}
A type is a Stone space if it merely is a sequential limit of finite types.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
This is equivalent to the definition of Stone spaces as spectrum of countably presented booolean algebras given in \cite{synthetic-stone-duality} by Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 from \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}. We use this alternative definition to emphasis the duality between Stone spaces and overtly discrete types. Indeed overtly discrete types will be defined as sequential colimits of finite types.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}\label{closed-iff-stone}
A proposition is a Stone space if and only if it is closed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Corollary 3.9 of \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
We have the following:

\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Finite types are Stone spaces.
\item Stone spaces are stable under identity types and and $\Sigma$-types.
\item Stone spaces are stable under sequential limits.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As follows:

\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Clear.
\item Lemma 3.10 and Theorem 4.18 of \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}.
\item From duality and the fact that a sequential colimit of a countably presented boolean algebra is countably presented.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Overtly discrete types}
\label{overtly-discrete-types}

Here we give generalities on overtly discrete types. Most results come from \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}.

\begin{definition}
A type is overtly discrete if it merely is a sequential colimit of finite types.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
A type is countable if it merely is a decidable subset of $\N$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{overtly-discrete-proposition-open}
A proposition is overtly discrete if and only if it is open.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is Lemma 2.8 of \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}.
\end{proof}

Next characterisation gives helpful intuitions about overtly discrete types. In some sense that are countably presented sets.

\begin{lemma}\label{overtly-discrete-colimit-finite}
Let $X$ be a type, the following are equivalent:

\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item $X$ is overtly discrete.
\item $X$ is a quotient of a countable type by an open equivalence relation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is Lemma 2.12 of \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}.
\end{proof}

Now we give the stability properties enjoyed by overtly discrete types. In Barton-Commelin's condensed type theory, a class of types called overtly discrete types enjoying these properties is assumed given.

\begin{proposition}\label{overtly-discrete-closure}
We have the following:

\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Finite types are overtly discrete.
\item Overtly discrete types are stable under identity types and $\Sigma$-types.
\item Overtly discrete type are stable under sequential colimits.
\item Overtly discrete types are stable under quotients by open equivalence relations.
\item Overtly discrete types have local choice.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As follows:

\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Clear.
\item This is Lemma 2.7 of \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}.
\item This is Lemma 2.6 of \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}.
\item This follows from \Cref{overtly-discrete-colimit-finite}.
\item By \cref{overtly-discrete-colimit-finite}, we have a cover of any overtly discrete type by a countable type, which is an overtly discrete type that has choice.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

%\begin{remark}
%By \Cref{overtly-discrete-proposition-open} we have that closure (iv) can be rephrased as overtly discrete being stable under quotient by equivalence relations with value in overtly discrete types. This is how this condition is phrased in Barton-Commelin's axioms.
%\end{remark}

Recall that \Cref{overtly-discrete-colimit-finite} means that overtly discrete types are in some sense countably presented sets. This extends nicely to algebraic structure.

\begin{lemma}\label{overtly-discrete-countably-presented}
A boolean algebra (resp.\ group, abelian group) is overtly discrete if and only if it is countably presented.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The case of boolean algebras is Corollary 2.14 of \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}. The case of groups and abelian groups is similar, the result actually holds for any finitary algebraic notion.
\end{proof}



\subsection{Scott continuity for Stone spaces}
\label{scott-continuity-for-cohomology}

Here we prove the results required for our cohomology computations, namely Propositions \ref{scott-continuity-right}, \ref{scott-continuity-left} and \ref{tychonov-dual-stone}. We then sum up these results nicely as \Cref{eine-scott-continuity-stone}, which is a dependent version of the following: 

\begin{goal}[Non-dependent version]
Given a tower of Stone spaces $(S_k)_{k:\N}$ and a tower of overtly discrete types $(I_j)_{j:\N}$, we have that the canonical map $\mathrm{colim}_{k,j}(S_k\to I_j) \to (\mathrm{lim}_k S_k\to \mathrm{colim}_j I_j)$ is an equivalence.
\end{goal}

\begin{remark}
We call this result and its generalisations Scott continuity as they immediately imply that any map in $2^\N = (\mathrm{lim}_k\, 2^k)\to \N$ factors through $2^k$ for some $k$, meaning any such map only use a fixed finite prefix of its input.
\end{remark}

First we deal with the case where $S_k$ is constant and the target is dependent. A key point is to show any map in $\Pi_{x:S}\, \mathrm{colim}_i\, I_i(x)$ with $S$ Stone and $I_i(x)$ overtly discrete factors through one of the $I_i$. To do this we will show it factors through $\mathrm{Im}(I_i)$ for some $i:\N$ and conclude from there. We start by an auxiliary lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{overtly-discrete-union-open}
Given a tower of overtly discrete types $(I_i)_{i:\N}$, for any $i:\N$ we have that $\mathrm{Im}(I_i)\subseteq\mathrm{colim}_i\, I_i$ is open. 
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We write $I$ for $\mathrm{colim}_i\, I_i$. For any $y:I$, we have that $y\in \mathrm{Im}(I_i)$ is by definition $\propTrunc{\Sigma_{x:I_i}\, x=_{I}y}$,
which is the propositional truncation of an overtly discrete type and therefore open.
\end{proof}

Now we show that factoring through the image is enough.

\begin{lemma}\label{factorisation-image-true-factorisation}
Assume given a Stone space $S$ and for each $x:S$ a tower of overtly discrete types $(I_i(x))_{i:\N}$. Given $f:\Pi_{x:S}\,\mathrm{colim}_i\, I_i(x)$ which factors through $\mathrm{Im}(I_i)$ for some $i:\N$, we gave that $f$ factors through $I_j$ for some $j\geq i$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We write $I(x)$ for $\mathrm{colim}_i\, I_i(x)$. By local choice there exists a surjective map $p:T\to S$
with $g:\Pi_{x:T}\, I_i(p(x))$ such that $[g(x)] =_{I(x)} f(p(x))$ for all $x:T$.
Then we have
\[\forall(x,y:T).\, (p(x)=p(y) \to [g(x)] =_{I(x)} [g(y)])\]
so that
\[ \{x,y:T\ |\ p(x)=p(y)\} \to \exists(i:\N). [g(x)] =_{I_i(x)} [g(y)].\]
Since $\{x,y:T\ |\ p(x)=p(y)\}$ is Stone, we can apply \Cref{compact-hausforff-countable-cover} to get a $j:\N$ such that:
\[\forall(x,y:T).\, (p(x)=p(y) \to [g(x)] =_{I_j(x)} [g(y)])\]
which gives a factorisation of $f$ through $I_j$.
\end{proof}

We can conclude in the case where the source is constant.

\begin{proposition}\label{scott-continuity-right}
Assume given a Stone space $S$ and for each $x:S$ a tower of overtly discrete types $(I_i(x))_{i:\N}$. Then the canonical map $\mathrm{colim}_i (\Pi_{x:S} I_i(x)) \to \Pi_{x:S} \mathrm{colim}_i I_i(x)$ is an equivalence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First we check the canonical map is injective. Given $f,g:\Pi_{x:S} I_i(x)$ such that
\[\forall(x:S).\, [f(x)] =_{\mathrm{colim}_iI_i(x)} [g(x)].\]
We have that
\[\forall(x:S).\, \exists(i:\N).\, f(x) =_{I_i(x)} g(x)\]
so by \Cref{compact-hausforff-countable-cover} there exist a $j:\N$ such that
\[\forall(x:S).\, [f(x)] =_{I_j(x)} [g(x)].\]
This precisely means that $f=g$ in $\mathrm{colim}_i\, \Pi_{x:S} I_i(x)$.

Now we check that the canonical map is surjective. Given a map $f: \Pi_{x:S}\, \mathrm{colim}_i\, I_i(x)$ we know that
\[\forall(x:S).\, \exists(i:\N).\, f(x)\in \mathrm{Im}(I_i(x)).\]
But by \Cref{overtly-discrete-union-open} we have that the proposition $f(x)\in \mathrm{Im}(I_i(x))$ is open, so by \Cref{compact-hausforff-countable-cover} there exists $j:\N$ such that:
\[\forall(x:S).\, f(x)\in \mathrm{Im}(I_j(x))\]
This precisely means that $f$ factors through $\mathrm{Im}(I_j)$. We conclude by \Cref{factorisation-image-true-factorisation}.
\end{proof}

Now we deal with the case where target is constant and non-dependent. If the target is finite this follows from duality.

\begin{lemma}\label{factorisation-stone-finite}
Assume given a tower of Stone spaces $(S_k)_{k:\N}$ and a finite type $I$. Then the canonical map $\mathrm{colim}_k\,(S_k\to I)\to \left(\mathrm{lim}_k\,S_k\to I\right)$ is an equivalence.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Indeed we have that
\begin{eqnarray}
\mathrm{colim}_k\,(S_k\to I) &=& \mathrm{colim}_k\, \Hom(2^I, 2^{S_k})\nonumber\\
&=& \Hom(2^I, \mathrm{colim}_k\,2^{S_k})\nonumber\\
&=& \Spec(\mathrm{colim}_k\,2^{S_k})\to \Spec(2^I) \nonumber\\
&=& \mathrm{lim}_k\,S_k\to I\nonumber
\end{eqnarray}
where the second line comes from the fact that $2^I$ is finitely presented. We omit the verification that this is indeed the canonical map.
\end{proof}

Now we extend to the case where the target is an arbitrary overtly discrete type.

\begin{proposition}\label{scott-continuity-left}
Assume given a tower of Stone spaces $(S_k)_{k:\N}$ and an overtly discrete type $I$. Then canonical map $\mathrm{colim}_k\,(S_k\to I)\to \left(\mathrm{lim}_k\,S_k\to I\right)$ is an equivalence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
There exists a tower of finite types $(I_i)_{i:\N}$ so that $I = \mathrm{colim}_i\,I_i$. By the non-dependent version of \Cref{scott-continuity-right} together with \Cref{factorisation-stone-finite} we have that
\begin{eqnarray}
\mathrm{colim}_k\,(S_k\to \mathrm{colim}_i\,I_i) &=& \mathrm{colim}_{k,i}\,(S_k\to I_i)\nonumber\\
&=& \mathrm{colim}_i\,(\mathrm{lim}_k\,S_k\to I_i)\nonumber\\
&=& \mathrm{lim}_k\,S_k\to \mathrm{colim}_i\,I_i.\nonumber
\end{eqnarray}
We omit the verification that this is indeed the canonical map.
\end{proof}

Now we prove the dual of Tychonov for Stone spaces, i.e.\ that a product of overtly discrete types indexed by a Stone space is itself overtly discrete. We need an auxiliary lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{tychonov-dual-auxiliary}
Assume given a surjection between Stone spaces $p:T\to S$ and for each $x:S$ an overtly discrete type $I(x)$. If $\Pi_{x:T}I(p(x))$ is overtly discrete then so is $\Pi_{x:S}I(x)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since the map is surjective we have an embedding $\Pi_{x:S}I(x)\subset \Pi_{x:T}I(p(x))$. But the fiber over $g:\Pi_{x:T}I(p(x))$ is
\[\forall (x,y:T).\, p(x)=p(y) \to g(x)=g(y)\]
which is open by \Cref{compact-hausdorff-compact}. Therefore $\Pi_{x:S}I(x)$ is an open subtype of an overtly discrete type, so it is itself overtly discrete.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Tychonov's Dual]\label{tychonov-dual-stone}
Assume given a Stone space $S$ and for each $x:S$ an overtly discrete type $I(x)$. Then $\Pi_{x:S}I(x)$ is overtly discrete.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By local choice and \Cref{tychonov-dual-auxiliary} we can assume given tower of finite types $(\mathrm{Fin}(l_{k,x}))_{k:\N}$
such that for all $x:S$ we have that $I(x) = \mathrm{colim}_k\, \mathrm{Fin}(l_{k,x})$.
Then using \Cref{scott-continuity-right} and the fact that overtly discrete types are stable under sequential colimits, it is enough to prove that $\Pi_{x:S} \mathrm{Fin}(l_{k,x})$ is overtly discrete. 

Using boundedness we have that there exists $n:\N$ such that $\Pi_{x:S} \mathrm{Fin}(l_{k,x}) = \Pi_{i<n}(S_i\to \mathrm{Fin}(i))$ where $S_i = \{x:S\ |\ l_{k,x} = i\}$. Then by \Cref{factorisation-stone-finite} we know that each $S_i\to \mathrm{Fin}(i)$ is overtly discrete, and we conclude using the fact that overtly discrete types are closed under finite products.
\end{proof}

So far we have two versions of Scott continuity (Propositions \ref{scott-continuity-right} and \ref{scott-continuity-left}), neither clearly implying the other. They are sufficient to derive our cohomology results. In the rest of this section we will prove a common generalisation suggested to us by Reid Barton. We start by an auxiliary definition.

\begin{definition}\label{category-scott-continuity}
We have a category ${\mathcal C}_\Stone$ defined by:

\begin{itemize}
\item An object consists of $S:\Stone$ and $I:S\to \ODisc$.
\item A morphism from $(S,I)$ to $(T,J)$ consists of $p:T\to S$ with $q:\Pi_{x:T}(I(p(x))\to J(x))$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The arrows are defined such that we have a covariant functor $\Pi:{\mathcal C}_\Stone\to \ODisc$. It is indeed valued in $\ODisc$ by \Cref{tychonov-dual-stone}. Now we prove the general version of Scott continuity for Stone spaces.

\end{theorem}

\subsection{Barton-Commelin's condensed type theory axioms}
\label{barton-commelin}

We already have proven all the results needed for our cohomology computations. In this section complete the proof that Barton-Commelin's condensed type theory axioms hold in synthetic Stone duality. These axioms postulate two classes of types (called compact Hausdorff spaces and overtly discrete types) such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item Both classes are closed under identity types, $\Sigma$-types, sequential limit (resp.\ colimit) and quotients.
\item Both classes satisfy local choice.
\item The classes interact well in the sense that they satisfy Tychonov, its dual and Scott continuity.
\end{itemize}

We have already defined the class of overtly discrete types and proved its required closure properties in \Cref{overtly-discrete-closure}. We did show that Stone spaces enjoy the required interaction results with overtly discrete types in \Cref{scott-continuity-for-cohomology}. But they are not closed under quotient. The solution is to define compact Hausdorff spaces as quotients of Stone spaces by closed equivalence relation, and then extend the interaction results from Stone spaces to compact Hausdorff spaces.

\begin{definition}
A type $C$ is a compact Hausdorff space of its identity types are closed and there exists a Stone space $S$ with a surjection $S\to C$.
\end{definition}

To prove that compact Hausdorff spaces are closed under sequential limits, we need the following auxiliary lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{sequential-limit-Hausdorff}
Assume given a tower of compact Hausdorff spaces $(C_k)_{k:\N}$. There exists a tower of Stone spaces $(S_k)_{k:\N}$ with a map of tower $(S_k\to C_k)_{k:\N}$ which is level-wise surjective, such that the induced map $\mathrm{lim}_k\,S_k\to \mathrm{lim}_k\,C_k$ is also surjective.

%\begin{center}
%\begin{tikzcd}
%\cdots \ar[r]& S_1\ar[r]\ar[d] & S_0 \ar[d]\\
%\cdots \ar[r] & C_1\ar[r] & C_0 \\
%\end{tikzcd}
%\end{center}

%such that the map:
%\[S_0\to C_0\]
%is surjective and for all $n:\N$ the induced map:
%\[S_{n+1} \to C_{n+1}\times_{C_n} S_n\]
%is surjective.

%This implies that the induced map:
%\[\lim_kS_k \to \lim_k C_k\]
%is surjective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We will build a tower of Stone spaces $(S_k)_{k:\N}$ and a map of tower $(S_k\to C_k)_{k:\N}$ such that the induced maps $S_{k+1}\to C_{k+1}\times_{C_k}S_k$ are surjective, which immediately implies the map level-wise surjective by induction on $k$, and implies the induced map $\mathrm{lim}_k\,S_k\to \mathrm{lim}_k\,C_k$ surjective by dependent choice.

For $k=0$ we can merely find a Stone space $S_0$ with a surjection $p_0:S_0\to C_0$. 

Next assume a surjection $p_k:S_k\to C_k$ and shows there merely exists a Stone space $S_{k+1}$ with a map $p_{k+1}:S_{k+1}\to C_{k+1}$ making the appropriate square commutes, such that the induced map $S_{k+1}\to C_{k+1}\times_{C_k} S_k$ is a surjection (and therefore $p_{k+1}$ is a surjection).
%Using dependent choice, it is enough to show that we can merely extend such a tower $(S_k)_{k\leq n}$ to $(S_k)_{k\leq n+1}$.

Indeed there exists a Stone space $T$ with a surjection $q:T \to C_{k+1}$. Then we define $S_{k+1}$ by the following pullback squares
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
S_{k+1}\ar[d,swap,"\pi_1"]\ar[r] & C_{k+1}\times_{C_k} S_k\ar[r]  \ar[d]& S_k\ar[d,"p_k"]\\
T \ar[r,swap,"q"] & C_{k+1}\ar[r]  & C_k
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
We define $p_{k+1} = q\circ \pi_1$. The induced map in $S_{k+1}\to C_{k+1}\times_{C_k} S_k$ is a surjection as it is a pullback of the surjection $q$. 

By dependent choice we get the required map of tower.
\end{proof}

We are now ready to prove the required closure properties for compact Hausdorff spaces.

\begin{proposition}
We have the following:

\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Finite types are compact Hausdorff spaces.
\item Compact Hausdorff spaces are stable under identity types and and $\Sigma$-types.
\item Compact Hausdorff spaces are stable under quotients by closed equivalence relations.
\item Compact Hausdorff spaces are stable under sequential limits.
\item Compact Hausdorff space have local choice.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We proceed as follows:

\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Clear.
\item By \Cref{closed-iff-stone} and Lemma 4.11 of \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}.

\item Clear from the definition of compact Hausdorff spaces as quotients.

\item Assume given a tower of compact Hausdorff spaces $(C_k)_{k:\N}$. Closed proposition are stable under sequential limits, so identity types in $\mathrm{lim}_k\,C_k$ are closed. By \cref{sequential-limit-Hausdorff} we get a surjection from a Stone space to $\mathrm{lim}_k\,C_k$,
\item Given a compact Hausdorff space $C$ there is a surjection $S\to C$ with $S$ Stone, and Stone spaces have local choice.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

Next we need to prove Tychonov and its dual for compact Hausdorff spaces.

\begin{proposition}[Tychonov]
Assume given an overtly discrete type $I$ and for each $i:I$ a compact Hausdorff space $C(i)$. Then $\Pi_{i:I}\,C(i)$ is a compact Hausdorff space.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We can assume $I = \mathrm{colim}_{k:\N}\,I_k$ with $I_k$ finite. Then $\Pi_{i:I}\,C(i) = \lim_{k:\N}\, \Pi_{i:I_k}\,C(\iota_k(i))$
and we can conclude using that compact Hausdorff spaces are stable under sequential limits and finite products.
\end{proof}

Extending the Tychonov's dual from Stone spaces to compact Hausdorff spaces only requires generic reasoning on quotients.

\begin{proposition}[Tychonov's dual]
Assume given a compact Hausdorff space $C$ and for each $x:X$ an overtly discrete type $I(x)$. Then $\Pi_{x:C}\,I(x)$ is an overtly discrete type.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We consider a surjection $p:S\to C$ with $S$ Stone. Then
\[\Pi_{x:C}\,I(x) = \{f:\Pi_{x:S}\,I(p(x))\ |\ \forall(x,y:S).(p(x)=p(y)\to f(x)=f(y))\}.\]
But $\forall(x,y:S).(p(x)=p(y)\to f(x)=f(y))$ is open by \Cref{compact-hausdorff-compact} and $\Pi_{x:S}\,I(p(x))$ is overtly discrete by \Cref{tychonov-dual-stone}, so we can conclude.
\end{proof}

Finally we extend Scott continuity to compact Hausdorff spaces. We write ${\mathcal C}_\CHaus$ for ${\mathcal C}_\Stone$ as given in \Cref{category-scott-continuity} with Stone spaces replaced by compact Hausdorff spaces. As for Tychonov's dual, this just involves generic reasoning on quotients.

\begin{theorem}[Scott Continuity]\label{scott-continuity-chaus}
The functor $\Pi : {\mathcal C}_\CHaus \to \ODisc$ commutes with sequential colimits.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume given a tower in ${\mathcal C}_\Chaus$ denoted by $(C_i,I_i)_{i:\N}$. By \Cref{sequential-limit-Hausdorff} we can consider a tower of Stone spaces $(S_i)_{i:\N}$ with a level-wise surjection $(p_i:S_i\to C_i)_{i:\N}$ to the tower $(C_i)_{i:\N}$. We write $S=\mathrm{lim}_i\,S_i$ and $C=\mathrm{lim}_i\,C_i$, for each $x:C$ we write $I(x) = \mathrm{colim}_i\,I_i(x_i)$ and finally we write $p =\mathrm{lim}_i\,p_i$. Then the canonical map
\[\mathrm{colim}_i(\Pi_{x:C_i}\,I_i(x) ) \to \Pi_{x:C}\,I(x)\]
is equal to the canonical map

\[ \left\{ \iota_i(f_i) : \mathrm{colim}_i\, \Pi_{x:S_i}\, I_i(p_i(x))\ |\ \mathrm{colim}_{j\geq i}\, Q_j(f_i)\right\} \longrightarrow \left\{f:\Pi_{x:S}\, I(p(x))\ |\ Q(f)\right\} \]
where we defined
\begin{eqnarray}
 Q_j(f_i) &=& \forall(x,y:S_j).\, p_j(x)=p_j(y)\to \iota_j(f_i(x_i))=\iota_j(f_i(y_i))\nonumber\\
Q(f) &=& \forall(x,y:S).\, p(x)=p(y)\to f(x)=f(y).\nonumber
\end{eqnarray}
Then by \Cref{eine-scott-continuity-stone} we have that the canonical map
\[\epsilon : \mathrm{colim}_i(\Pi_{x:S_i}\,I_i(p_i(x))) \to \Pi_{x:S}\, I(p(x))\]
is an equivalence, and by \Cref{eine-scott-continuity-stone} again we have that the canonical map
\[\mathrm{colim}_{j\geq i}\,Q_j(f_i) \to Q(\epsilon(\iota_i(f_i)))\]
is an equivalence as well so we can conclude.
\end{proof}
