The topologist's sine curve $\mathcal S$ is a space that distinguishes the notions of being connected and being path-connected. In this note, we show that $\mathcal S$ can be defined as a compact Hausdorff space in the setting of synthetic Stone duality (SSD) as introduced in \cite{synthetic-stone-duality}. 
We show that $\mathcal S$ is still not path-connected in this setting. However, we show it is $[0,1]$-contractible. 
It is known that any inhabited path-connected type is $[0,1]$-contractible, but the converse thus does not hold. 
This means that localization at the unit interval is not the same as taking path-connected components. 
We were inspired to study the space by looking at this localization by \cite{shulman-Brouwer-fixed-point}.
