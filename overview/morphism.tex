Without much further effort, in synthetic algebraic geometry, we can only make relative definitions. However, it turns out, that many definitions are easier synthetically. There, all codomain-local morpism properties can be formulated as scheme-theoretic properties of the fibers of a morphism.
So far, we have the definitions of (local) morphism properties listed below. References refer to either a definition of the morphism property of a property of types/schemes that is supposed to be applied to the fibers of a morphism. Schemes in synthetic algebraic geometry are always assumed to be quasi-compact, quasi-separated and locally of finite presentation.
\begin{center}
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Affine \cite{draft}
\item Projective \cite{proper-draft}
\item Finite \cite{finite-draft}, finite free
\item Proper \cite{proper-draft}
\item Flat
\item Reduced
\item Separated
\item (Formally) étale, smooth (of dimension n), unramified \cite{diffgeo-article}
\item Étale-overt, overt (we do not know a classic counterpart) \cite{27-lines-draft}
\end{enumerate}  
\end{center}
In synthetic algebraic geometry we tend to show closure properties for type formers like $\Sigma$ instead of category theoretical constructions like pullbacks. Closure under $\Sigma$ and $\_=\_$ imply closure under pullbacks.
We have the following closure properties:
\begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    Property & Closed under \\
    Affine & $\Sigma$, $\_=\_$, closed subsets \\
    Scheme & $\Sigma$, $\_=\_$, closed subsets, open subsets \\
    Finite & $\Sigma$, $\_=\_$, closed subsets \\
    Projective & $\_=\_$, $\_\times\_$, closed subsets \\
    Proper & $\Sigma$, closed subsets, Zariski-images \\
    Reduced & $\Sigma$, Zariski-images \\
    
    Formally étale & modality: $\Sigma$, $\_=\_$, $\prod$ with arbitrary base \\ 
    Formally unramified & modality: $\Sigma$, $\_=\_$, $\prod$ with arbitrary base \\
    Formally smooth & $\Sigma$, Zariski-images \\
    Étale-overt & $\Sigma$, Zariski-images, étale-images \\
  \end{tabular}
\end{center}
